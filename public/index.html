    1. Oct 2023

      The idea

      A friend pitched me a marketplace where supplement brands would compete on nutrition instead of vibes. It started as a side project.

    2. Mar 2024

      NutraStack Inc. is incorporated

      From here until November, NutraStack was close to a full-time job.

      Sorting and filtering came first, then side-by-side compare, proprietary blends broken into their ingredients, and a landing page with a waitlist.

    4. Jul–Aug 2024

      Every product got a Buy link that routed to the brand or to Amazon. Category rankings, search and sign-up followed, along with partner discount codes and referral links.

    What we built

    Four ways to shop by the label

    Every product was stored as its full Supplement or Nutrition Facts panel, one row per ingredient and dose. Everything else came from that data.

    Shipped · Rank

    Discover

    66 categories, from high-stim pre-workouts to frozen desserts. Sort by up to 10 criteria at once, such as creatine per dollar, and filter out what you don't want, like added sugar or artificial flavors.

    Shipped · Compare

    Compare

    Up to three products side by side, ingredient by ingredient, with blends expanded and flavors you could switch between.

    Shipped · Inspect

    Product pages

    The full label for every flavor, % Daily Value, and a percentile showing how each dose compared with the rest of the category.

    Shipped · Learn

    Ingredients

    1,880 tracked ingredients, each with an article explaining what it does and a ranking of the products that contain the most of it.

    Shelved

    • StacksBuild a named stack of products and share it with an affiliate link. Moved to beta in July 2024 and never launched.
    • ProfilesUsernames, follows, likes and views around shared stacks.
    • Creator linksPer-user affiliate links so creators could earn from the stacks they shared.

    For brands

    How a brand joined

    The other side of the marketplace. Our brand deck promised that products would rank on relevance, not ad budget, and that we only earned when a brand made a sale.

    Listing

    We added the brand's products for them: images, nutrition labels, descriptions and prices.

    Marketing

    Featured brands appeared in our social posts and the landing page carousel.

    Commission

    A flat 20% on referred sales, open to negotiation.

    Payouts

    Monthly through PayPal, or Venmo, direct deposit or Shopify Collabs.

    Tracking

    A referral URL with a 30 to 60 day cookie, plus a discount code such as NUTRASTACK10 where tracking wasn't possible.

    Under the hood

    What we built underneath

    • A Flask app on Azure App Service, deployed from GitHub Actions, backed by Azure SQL.
    • A hand-built label database. Serving size, servings per container, every ingredient and dose, and the other-ingredients list for each flavor, with 176 proprietary blends mapped to their components.
    • Weighted ranking. Each criterion was normalized, passed through a sigmoid fit to that category's spread, weighted 1 to 5 and summed. Cost per serving was always one of the criteria.
    • Affiliate routing. The Buy button resolved to a partner's direct link, a referral parameter or an Amazon Associates link, and logged every click.
    • First-party analytics for sessions, page views, searches, comparisons and outbound clicks, plus server errors posted to Discord.

    Why it stopped

    The work outgrew the returns

    NutraStack ran on data. Every flavor of every product was a full label, entered and checked by hand, and every new brand, flavor or reformulation meant more of it. Through most of 2024 that was more than ten hours a day.

    The returns didn't follow. $5,689 of Instagram and TikTok ads grew the accounts to 1,191 and 1,652 followers, about $2 per follower, but followers didn't become shoppers. Sales through the site were minimal, and a 20% commission on minimal sales never came close to paying for the work.

    In 2025 I pitched a few more brands, but I wasn't willing to go back to 10-hour days without a clear sign that the result would be different.

    Lessons

    What I'd tell myself in 2023

    1. Followers aren't shoppers

      We were already logging every outbound click. We should have spent $500 per channel, measured clicks and purchases, and stopped as soon as one didn't convert. Instead we bought reach and counted followers.

    2. Brands were the easy side

      Brands said yes far more easily than shoppers did. Getting shoppers was the actual business, and affiliate revenue needs volume before it's worth anything. I'd pick one niche, like pre-workouts, and win search traffic for it before adding a 66th category.

    3. Price the upkeep before you grow the catalog

      A label database is never finished. Brands reformulate, add flavors and change prices, and each of 108 brands added to the work. I should have automated label capture early, or kept the catalog to the categories that actually sold.
